Part A: The wobble

1. In the barycenter waltz, who moved in the bigger circle: the lighter person or the heavier person? Why?

2. When the whistling phone swung toward you, the pitch went ______ . When it swung away, the pitch went ______ .

3. A star's light does the same thing. When the star moves toward us, its spectral lines shift toward the ______ end of the spectrum. When it moves away, they shift toward the ______ end.

4. Jupiter makes the Sun wobble at about 12.5 m/s. Earth makes it wobble at about 0.09 m/s. Which planet would aliens find first by watching the Sun's wobble?

Part B: Weigh the planets

Open the Wobble Fitter. Do 51 Pegasi as a class, then your Transit Detective planet. If your Transit Detective star was C (LHS 3844), do pi Mensae instead. If it was F (the impostor), do WASP-62.

For each planet, adjust the period, the wobble speed K, and the left/right slider until the fit meter says Excellent.

Part C: What is it made of?

Type your Transit Detective radius into the Wobble Fitter to place your planet on the mass–radius diagram. For reference: water is 1.0 g/cm³, Jupiter 1.3, rock 3–5.5 (Earth is 5.5), iron about 8–11.

3. Where does your planet sit on the diagram? Which Solar System planet is it most like?

4. WASP-18 b and WASP-62 b are almost the same size. Compare their masses and densities. Which one would float in a (very large) bathtub?

5. Why do we need both a transit and a wobble to know what a planet is made of?

Part E: Exit ticket

Two planets have the same radius and orbit similar stars with similar periods. Planet X makes its star wobble at 1,800 m/s. Planet Y makes its star wobble at 60 m/s. Which planet is denser, and about how many times denser?
