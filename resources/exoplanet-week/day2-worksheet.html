Wobble Fitter Worksheet: Weighing Worlds

This worksheet goes with the Wobble Fitter activity.

Part A: Explore the wobble

Open the Wobble Fitter from the link your instructor shares. Start with Step by step: from wobble to mass, then use the RV Explorer to answer these.

1. Make the planet heavier. What happens to the star's wobble speed K? Why?

2. Keep the planet's mass the same and make its orbital period longer. What happens to K? Why?

3. When the star moves toward us, its spectral lines shift toward the ______ end of the spectrum. When it moves away, they shift toward the ______ end.

4. Try the "Jupiter around the Sun" and "Earth around the Sun" buttons. Write down K for each: Jupiter ______ m/s, Earth ______ m/s. Switch between telescopes. Which can detect each planet? Which planet would aliens find first by watching the Sun?

5. Tilt the orbit until we see it face-on. What happens to the wobble we measure? Why do astronomers say radial velocity gives a minimum mass?

Part B: Weigh the planets

With your partner, work through all five planets below in order. Each button in the Wobble Fitter says which Transit Detective star it was, so take each planet's radius from your Transit Detective table, or use the “Use the published radius” button.

For each planet, adjust the period, the wobble speed K, and the left/right slider until the fit meter says Excellent.

6. 51 Peg b doesn't transit. How did you find its period without any transits?

7. For the same period, what happens to the planet's mass when K doubles?

Bonus: the Mystery star. Fit its big wobble, then look at the leftovers plot under the graph. If a wave is still there, press "Lock in this planet, then fit the leftovers" and fit it too. Planet 1: period ______ days, mass ______. Planet 2: period ______ days, mass ______. How did the leftovers tell you a second planet was there?

Part C: What is it made of?

Type each planet's radius (from your Transit Detective table or the published value) into the Wobble Fitter to place it on the mass–radius diagram. For reference: water is 1.0 g/cm³, Jupiter 1.3, rock 3–5.5 (Earth is 5.5), iron about 8–11.

8. Which of your planets is most like Jupiter? Which is most like Neptune or Earth? How can you tell from the diagram?

9. WASP-18 b and WASP-62 b are almost the same size. Compare their masses and densities. Which one would float in a (very large) bathtub?

10. Why do we need both a transit and a wobble to know what a planet is made of?

Part D: Why haven't we found Earth 2.0?

Open "Sun (seen by aliens)" in the Wobble Fitter and try to find Earth. Then look at the discovery map at the bottom of the page.

Part E: Putting it together

Two planets have the same radius and orbit similar stars with similar periods. Planet X makes its star wobble at 1,800 m/s. Planet Y makes its star wobble at 60 m/s. Which planet is denser, and about how many times denser?
